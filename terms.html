These terms cover using Plainscript. The most important part is the first section, and it is not boilerplate — please read it.

Plainscript is not medical advice

Plainscript is an educational reference. It explains what official drug labels say and flags documented interactions. It is not a doctor, a pharmacist, or a clinical tool, and using it is not a substitute for asking one.

Never start, stop, or change a medication based on what Plainscript shows you. Talk to a pharmacist or prescriber first. They can see your full history; Plainscript cannot.

In an emergency, call 911. For a suspected poisoning or overdose, call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222. Do not spend time in this app during an emergency.

1. What Plainscript deliberately will not do

These are design decisions, not missing features. Knowing what a tool refuses to do is part of using it safely. Plainscript will not:

  • Diagnose anything. It will not take your symptoms and suggest what condition you have. The Symptoms section maps everyday complaints to common over-the-counter categories and lists emergency warning signs — that is all it does.
  • Calculate or recommend a dose. It shows dosing text exactly as printed on the FDA label, and nothing more.
  • Tell you whether you personally should take something. It has no idea about your history, your other conditions, or your kidney and liver function.
  • Tell you a combination is safe. When it finds nothing, it says it found nothing. That is not the same statement as "this is safe," and it is never shown as one.
  • Handle an emergency. It routes you to 911 and Poison Control instead.

2. Accuracy, sources, and limits

Plainscript draws its facts from the FDA's published drug labels, the National Library of Medicine's RxNorm database, and a hand-checked set of documented interactions. Every claim shown carries its source. The AI feature only rewrites or quotes label text that has already been retrieved — it is not allowed to write medical claims of its own.

Even so, understand the limits:

  • Coverage is incomplete. The curated interaction set covers well-documented interactions, not every interaction that exists. No free, comprehensive drug-interaction database has been publicly available since the NIH retired its interaction API in January 2024. A pharmacist has tools Plainscript does not.
  • Labels can be out of date or incomplete relative to the newest research, and some products have no usable label data at all.
  • Lookups can fail. If a data source is unreachable, Plainscript tells you the result is incomplete rather than pretending it checked. Do not read a failed lookup as an all-clear.
  • Plain-language rewriting can lose nuance. The original label text is always one tap away, and it is the authority.

If you spot something wrong, please report it — there is a link in the app footer. Corrections are taken seriously.

Plainscript is a general-audience tool and is not directed at children under 13. If you create an account, you confirm you are old enough to do so where you live, and that the information you enter is either your own or that of someone you are genuinely helping to care for.

4. Accounts and your cabinet

  • An account is optional. Decoding drugs and checking interactions work without one.
  • You are responsible for keeping access to your own email or Google account secure, since that is how signing in works.
  • You are responsible for what you save. If you track medications for another person, make sure that is something they would be comfortable with.
  • Share links are unlisted but unprotected — anyone holding the link can view that cabinet. Send them only to people you intend to have them, and revoke them when you are done.
  • You can delete individual entries yourself at any time. Full account deletion is currently handled manually on request — see the Privacy Policy for how.

5. Acceptable use

Use Plainscript for its purpose: understanding medications. Please do not:

  • Present its output as professional medical advice, or embed it in anything that does.
  • Use it to help anyone misuse, obtain, or combine drugs in a harmful way.
  • Attempt to break, overload, or abuse the service or its underlying APIs, including automated scraping or hammering the AI endpoint.
  • Try to reach accounts or cabinets that are not yours, or guess share tokens.
  • Copy the interaction data and republish it as authoritative medical guidance somewhere else.

Plainscript's source code is public. You are welcome to read it, learn from it, and check the safety claims made here against it.
