Plainscript

Privacy Policy

Effective 16 September 2026

Plainscript is a free, educational medication reference. This page explains exactly what it stores, what it never stores, and who else sees your information. It is written to be read, not to be skimmed past.

The short version

  • You can use Decode and the interaction checker without an account. If you never sign in, Plainscript stores nothing about you on its servers.
  • If you do sign in, it stores your email address and the medications you choose to save.
  • The medications you save are health information. They are treated as sensitive. They are never sold, never used for advertising, and never shared for anyone else's marketing.
  • There are no analytics, no ad trackers, and no third-party tracking scripts anywhere in Plainscript.
  • Deleting your data is not yet self-service. You have to ask, and the request is handled manually. See Your rights.

1. What Plainscript collects

If you never sign in

Nothing about you is stored on Plainscript's servers. Searching a drug and checking interactions both work signed out, and neither creates a record tied to you. Your preferences (theme, language, reading mode) are saved in your own browser only.

This does not mean nobody sees anything. Looking up a drug sends that drug's name directly from your browser to the FDA and NIH, and loading any page contacts the services that host it. See Who else receives data.

If you create an account

Accounts are optional and exist for one reason: to save your medication list so it is there next time. Signing in is either a Google sign-in or a passwordless email link. When you have an account, the following is stored in Plainscript's database (hosted by Supabase):

2. Consumer health data

This section is a dedicated disclosure about health information, provided because the medications a person takes are sensitive, and because several state laws — including the Washington My Health My Data Act, Nevada's SB 370, and Connecticut's consumer health data provisions — treat this category of data specially even when the service is free and is not a doctor's office, hospital, or insurer. Plainscript is not a healthcare provider and is not covered by HIPAA. That is precisely why these consumer protections matter here.

What consumer health data is collected

Only what you deliberately save or type: the medications in your cabinet, your notes and dosing times for them, and — kept on your device only — any allergies you enter on the wallet card. Drug searches you run without saving anything are not recorded by Plainscript.

Where it comes from

From you, directly. Plainscript does not buy, receive, or infer health information about you from anyone else. It does not build a profile of you.

Why it is collected

To do the one thing you asked it to do: keep your list and check it for documented interactions. It is not used for advertising, profiling, scoring, research, training any AI model, or any secondary purpose.

Who it is shared with

Nobody, except the infrastructure needed to run the service (see the next section) and anyone you personally hand a share link to. Plainscript does not sell consumer health data, and does not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising. There is no advertising in Plainscript at all.

Your consent, and withdrawing it

Nothing about your health is collected until you choose to save it. Creating an account and saving a medication are both deliberate actions, and either one is your consent to store what you saved. You can withdraw that consent at any time: delete individual medications yourself from within My Cabinet, revoke any share link you created, or request full deletion of your account and everything in it as described under Your rights. Withdrawing consent does not affect anything already done before you withdrew it.

3. Who else receives data

Plainscript is built so that most lookups happen straight from your browser rather than through a middleman server. That is good for speed and means Plainscript itself never logs your searches — but it also means those services see your requests and your IP address directly. Being honest about that matters more than sounding private.

WhoWhat they receiveWhen

Anthropic The excerpt of official FDA label text being rewritten, and — for "Ask the label" — the question you typed. Only when you turn on plain-English mode or ask a question about a label.

About the plain-English feature. It runs through a small server that holds the API key, so your browser never carries it. That server does not keep a copy of what passes through it. What is sent is the FDA label text plus your typed question, and nothing else — not your email, not your account, not your cabinet, and not your notes. The feature can only rewrite or quote the label text it is given; it cannot be used to generate medical claims.

4. What Plainscript does not do

  • No analytics. There is no Google Analytics, no page-view counter, no session recording, no heatmaps.
  • No advertising and no ad trackers. No third-party advertising or tracking script is loaded anywhere.
  • No selling or renting your data. Not to data brokers, not to pharmacies, not to anyone.
  • No marketing email. Your email address is used to sign you in, and for nothing else.
  • No AI training on your data. Your cabinet, notes, and account are never sent to any AI service.

5. How long things are kept

  • Your account and cabinet: kept until you delete them. There is no automatic expiry — an untouched account keeps its data until deletion is requested.
  • Medications you delete: removed from the database when you delete them.
  • Share links: the record stays until the account is deleted, but a revoked link stops working immediately.
  • Data on your device: stays until you clear your browser data or sign out.
  • Plain-English requests: not stored by Plainscript at all. Anthropic's own retention is governed by their policies.

6. Your rights, and how to actually use them

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to know what is held about you, get a copy of it, correct it, delete it, withdraw consent, and not be discriminated against for exercising any of those. Californian residents have these rights under the CCPA/CPRA, including specific rights over sensitive personal information, a category that includes health information. Residents of Washington, Nevada, Connecticut and a growing number of other states have comparable rights over consumer health data. Plainscript honours these requests regardless of which state you live in.

What you can do yourself, right now

  • See everything held about you: it is what you see in My Cabinet, plus your email address and your theme and language settings.
  • Delete any medication, note, or dosing time: remove it in My Cabinet and it is deleted from the database.
  • Revoke a share link: use the revoke button in the share panel. It stops working immediately.
  • Erase everything on a device: sign out, then clear your browser's site data for Plainscript.

Deleting your whole account — read this part carefully

Full account deletion is not yet a button in the app. It is handled manually, by hand, on request. That is an honest limitation, not a policy choice, and a self-service delete control is a planned addition.

To request deletion of your account and everything stored with it, open a request at the project's issue tracker: github.com/privi-doughnut/plainscript/issues. That tracker is public, so do not post your email address or any medication details in it — say only that you are requesting deletion, and you will be told where to send the address privately.

7. Share links

My Cabinet can generate a link that shows your medication list, read-only, to someone without an account — meant for a caregiver or a family member. If you use it, understand how it behaves:

  • The link contains a long random token. It cannot realistically be guessed.
  • Anyone holding the link can view that list. There is no password on it. Treat it like a key, and send it only to someone you intend to have it.
  • A shared view shows only the generic name, brand name, drug class, and your notes. It does not expose your email address, your account ID, or anything else about you.
  • You can revoke the link at any time, and it stops working immediately.
  • Only you can create or revoke links for your own cabinet.

8. Security

No system is perfectly secure, and anyone claiming otherwise is selling something. What is actually in place:

  • Row-level security on every table, enforced by the database itself. A signed-in user can only ever read or change rows belonging to their own account — that rule is applied at the database, not just in the app.
  • Signed-out visitors have no direct database access at all. A share link is served through a single narrow function that accepts one token and returns only that cabinet's medication fields.
  • The AI API key is never in the browser. It exists only as a secret on the server that proxies those requests.
  • All traffic is over HTTPS.
  • The public key used by the app to reach the database is public by design, and is only as powerful as the row-level rules above allow.

If you find a security problem, please report it through the issue tracker rather than publicising it.

9. Children

Plainscript is a general-audience reference tool. It is not directed at children under 13, it is not marketed to them, and it does not knowingly collect information from them. If you believe a child under 13 has created an account, please get in touch and it will be deleted.
